# Design: Gradient Cancellation Proof Notebook

**Date:** 2026-04-22
**Goal:** Prove that when one ReLU neuron is pushed toward the positive half-space and one toward the negative half-space, their combined gradient contribution is exactly zero under any symmetric data distribution and an even target — and demonstrate that this collapses the effective representational capacity of an m-neuron network to m-2.

---

## Claim

For an antipodal neuron pair $(w_+, w_- = -w_+)$ with equal output weights $(v_+ = v_- = v)$, bias $(b_+ = b_- = 0)$, and input $x$ drawn from any zero-mean symmetric distribution:

$$\nabla_{w_+} L + \nabla_{w_-} L = v \cdot \mathbb{E}[(f(x) - y^*(x)) \cdot x]$$

When $y^*$ is even and $f$ is even (which holds by the symmetric construction of the pair), then $f - y^*$ is even, and $\mathbb{E}[\text{even}(x) \cdot x] = 0$ under any symmetric distribution. Therefore the combined gradient is identically zero — regardless of the specific distribution shape.

**Consequence:** The pair is frozen in function space. They can only scale their current even contribution, not reshape it. The remaining $m - 2$ neurons must handle the full task alone.

---

## Target Function

$$y^*(x) = |x| + \text{relu}(|x| - 0.3) + \text{relu}(|x| - 0.7)$$

### Why this target

- **Even:** $y^*(-x) = y^*(x)$ — required for exact gradient cancellation.
- **Piecewise linear:** exactly representable by ReLU networks with no approximation error.
- **5 distinct kinks** at $\{-0.7,\,-0.3,\,0,\,+0.3,\,+0.7\}$ — any ReLU network with $\le 4$ neurons has at most 4 kinks and therefore cannot represent $y^*$ exactly.
- **Generated by exactly 6 neurons** arranged as 3 symmetric pairs:

| pair | $w_k$ | $b_k$ | $v_k$ |
|------|--------|--------|--------|
| 1 | $+1,\ -1$ | $0,\ 0$ | $1,\ 1$ |
| 2 | $+1,\ -1$ | $-0.3,\ -0.3$ | $1,\ 1$ |
| 3 | $+1,\ -1$ | $-0.7,\ -0.7$ | $1,\ 1$ |

---

## Architecture

- **Input:** 1D scalar $x$
- **Network:** $f_\theta(x) = \sum_{k=1}^{6} v_k\,\text{relu}(w_k x + b_k)$
- **Parameters:** $w_k,\, b_k,\, v_k$ for each of the 6 neurons (18 scalars total)
- **Data:** $x \sim \text{Uniform}(-1, 1)$, batch size 4096
- **Loss:** MSE — $L = \mathbb{E}[(f(x) - y^*(x))^2]$
- **Optimiser (Parts B & C):** Adam, lr = $10^{-3}$

---

## Part A — Algebraic Proof + Autograd Verification

**Setup:**
- Fix the antipodal pair: $w_+ = a$, $w_- = -a$, $b_+ = b_- = 0$, $v_+ = v_- = 1$.
- Fix the remaining 4 neurons at the analytically optimal positions from the target (pairs 2 and 3 from the table above).
- Compute $f(x)$ and $y^*(x)$ on a large uniform batch.

**Steps:**
1. State and derive the identity $\nabla_{w_+} L + \nabla_{w_-} L = v \cdot \mathbb{E}[(f - y^*) \cdot x]$ in a markdown cell with full derivation.
2. Show $f - y^*$ is even: display $f(x)$ and $f(-x)$ numerically to confirm symmetry.
3. Show $\mathbb{E}[\text{even}(x) \cdot x] = 0$: plot $(f(x) - y^*(x)) \cdot x$ vs $x$ to show it is an odd function with zero expectation.
4. Compute $\nabla_{w_+}$ and $\nabla_{w_-}$ with `torch.autograd.grad`.
5. **Sweep** $a \in \{0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.7, 0.9\}$: tabulate $|\nabla_{w_+} + \nabla_{w_-}|$ — must be $< 10^{-12}$ in all cases.
6. **Plot:** $\nabla_{w_+}$ vs $a$ and $\nabla_{w_-}$ vs $a$ on the same axis — they should be exact mirror images.

**Expected result:** Gradient sum $\approx 10^{-14}$ (machine zero) for every value of $a$.

## Part B — Frozen Pair + Capacity Argument

**Setup:**
- Freeze neurons 1–2 as the antipodal pair: $w_1 = 0.5,\, w_2 = -0.5,\, b_1 = b_2 = 0,\, v_1 = v_2 = 1$ (fixed, no gradient).
- Initialise neurons 3–6 randomly (Kaiming uniform).
- Train neurons 3–6 for $10^4$ Adam steps.

**Tracked quantities:**
- Loss over steps.
- Gradient norm of the frozen pair's parameters (computed via autograd even though not updated — must stay $\approx 0$).
- Gradient norm of the free neurons 3–6.
- Best achievable loss with 4 free neurons (lower bound: non-zero because 4 neurons cannot represent 5 kinks).

**Counterfactual (same checkpoint, additional $10^3$ steps):**
- Unfreeze all 6 neurons.
- Train until convergence.
- Loss must reach $< 10^{-6}$, proving the prior stagnation was capacity-driven.

**Plots:**
- Panel (a): loss vs step (plateau region + counterfactual drop).
- Panel (b): gradient norm of antipodal pair vs step (flat at $\sim 0$) vs free neurons (decaying).
- Panel (c): final $f(x)$ vs $y^*(x)$ at the plateau (show the mismatch at the 5th kink).
- Panel (d): final $f(x)$ vs $y^*(x)$ after unfreezing (near-perfect fit).

## Part C — Full LoP Replay (End-to-End)

**Phase 1 — Positive loading (3k steps):**
- Target: $y_{\text{pos}}(x) = \text{relu}(x)$.
- Train all 6 neurons. Asymmetric ReLU pressure drives some neurons toward $w_k > 0$.

**Phase 2 — Negative loading (3k steps):**
- Target: $y_{\text{neg}}(x) = \text{relu}(-x)$.
- Asymmetric pressure drives complementary neurons toward $w_k < 0$.
- By the end of Phase 2, at least one neuron should have $w > 0$ and one $w < 0$ with small activations in the opposite half — an organic antipodal pair.

**Pair identification (between phases):**
- After Phase 2, sort neurons by $w_k$. Take the neuron with the largest positive $w_k$ and the neuron with the largest negative $w_k$ as the "organic antipodal pair" for tracking purposes. Confirm that $|w_{\max} + w_{\min}| / |w_{\max}|$ is small (pair is approximately antipodal) before proceeding.

**Phase 3 — Trap (3k steps):**
- Target switches to the even $y^*(x)$.
- Track: gradient norms per neuron, loss.

**Baseline comparison:**
- Train a fresh 6-neuron network from random init directly on $y^*$ for 3k steps.
- Overlay the loss curve on Phase 3 to show the loaded network stagnates while the fresh one converges.

**Plots (matching the experiment style):**
- Panel (a): loss across all three phases (vertical lines separating phases).
- Panel (b): per-neuron gradient norms — collapse visible at Phase 3 start for the antipodal pair.
- Panel (c): weight trajectory of the two "rotating" neurons ($w_k$ vs step), showing rotation into the $\pm$ half-spaces during loading.
- Panel (d): final function fit at end of Phase 3 vs $y^*$, overlaid with the fresh-network fit.
