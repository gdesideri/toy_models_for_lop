# Predict when task geometry changes plasticity

**I would finish this project around one question: can the geometry of future tasks predict when a plasticity intervention will help, fail, or damage useful transfer?** The target contribution is a prospective, experimentally validated intervention-response boundary. The existing work supplies precise toy instrumentation, reusable checkpoints, and valuable negative controls; it does not yet establish that boundary. I would spend the first **1–2 weeks** trying to falsify this direction, then commit to a focused **6–10-week research program conditional on the pilot succeeding**. With ample compute, the priority is independent confirmation across architectures and task families, rather than expanding every existing sweep. A paper showing the boundary, its causal mechanism, and its usefulness on image tasks would justify an ICML/NeurIPS/ICLR submission. Another paper attributing plasticity loss generally to dead units, norm growth, or rank reduction would face serious novelty problems.

## The strongest evidence is finite-pool failure, alongside image-task improvement

The local audit counts **2,090 completed image-task histories**, but the substantial streams use width-30 MLPs and one seed per condition. Their recurring tasks mostly improve. In the 600-task CIFAR cluster baseline, all six factors improve between their first and last five appearances. Its only local health cache records **zero dead units in both hidden layers**, while last-layer effective rank falls from **18.79 to 12.65**. These observations neither demonstrate general plasticity loss nor establish that future unseen tasks remain learnable. They make an excellent negative control: recurrence accuracy and global rank cannot answer the proposed question. Shapes3D results here use learned cluster factors, not its true generative factors. ([Evidence audit and raw-run links](</Users/ariv18uq/Desktop/toy_models_for_lop/research_notes/Plasticity project publication strategy/evidence_audit.md>))

The cleanest positive result is a four-unit, trainable-bias ReLU toy trained on a fixed pool of 2,048 examples. Its phase-end live counts fall **4 → 2 → 1 → 0**; at phase 3, continued test accuracy is **49.71%**, versus **99.80%** for a fresh model. Every hidden feature is zero on every training example and the corresponding empirical gradients vanish. Yet **all four units retain positive maximum preactivation somewhere on the population support**; a few held-out examples activate them. This demonstrates absorption on the finite training pool, rather than loss of all population-accessible capacity. It motivates a question about future activation coverage; it does not establish that fresh sampling will rescue the model within a useful budget. ([Mechanism audit, reconstruction, and raw arrays](</Users/ariv18uq/Desktop/toy_models_for_lop/research_notes/Plasticity project publication strategy/mechanism_audit.md>))

Two tempting narratives should be dropped. The thin-x1/thin-x3 comparison changes labels, conditioning, and initial functions as well as support geometry, so it is not a clean coverage intervention. The antipodal theorem in the April plan is false: opposite parameter gradients can sum to zero while both neurons move and the represented function changes. Keep the valid fixed-pool absorption lemma and exact support calculation as interpretive tools, with their SGD and architectural assumptions explicit. They are supporting mathematics, not the contribution. ([Audit of both claims](</Users/ariv18uq/Desktop/toy_models_for_lop/research_notes/Plasticity project publication strategy/mechanism_audit.md>))

## Novelty requires predicting a causal response before observing it

The closest prior work already covers much of the proposed mechanism. Lyle et al. analyze preactivation drift, offsets, normalization, leaky ReLU, multiple independent causes, and revival after suitable input shifts. ([Disentangling the causes](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.18762)) ICML 2026 work already relates finite-budget learning to new-task residuals projected onto fast Gram eigendirections. ([Spectral collapse, Section 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.22335)) A May 2026 preprint falsifies rank-based diagnostics and predicts future optimization gains using optimization readiness. ([Predicting plasticity](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09044v1)) Invariant frozen/cloned-unit traps and tentative versus permanent ReLU death are also established topics. ([Barriers](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.00304v2); [Trainability of ReLU networks](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.09696))

**The proposed hypothesis is narrower and more useful:** a history creates task-dependent access to trainable features, and future input coverage together with required decision directions determines the response to repair. The strongest finding would be a predicted intervention reversal at the same checkpoint: repair improves adaptation to one future task but worsens adaptation to another of matched fresh difficulty. A sharp, generalizing rescue boundary could suffice without a sign reversal, provided it adds information beyond spectral alignment and optimization readiness. A correlation between activation probability and performance would not suffice.

For the ellipsoid toy, the exact support margin is

\[
m_i=w_i^\top\mu+\|A^\top w_i\|+b_i.
\]

It distinguishes population inactivity from sample silence. Estimate future activation probabilities \(p_i(C)=\Pr_C[z_i(x)>0]\) on independent inputs and combine them with measures of whether reachable features support the prospective target. The research task is to discover and validate an intervention-response rule using these quantities, not rename the support-function identity. On images, report measured coverage and uncertainty rather than claiming population certification. In deeper networks, upstream changes can reopen gates, so track coverage during adaptation. ([Exact local geometry](/Users/ariv18uq/Desktop/toy_models_for_lop/plasticity/src/unit_geometry.py))

## A paired checkpoint experiment must separate access, optimization, and difficulty

Use the existing orthogonal runner and fresh-model probes to branch identical learned states; checkpoint branching itself is infrastructure. The PCA A–B–C protocol provides a design for selecting harmful histories, rather than an achieved result. ([Orthogonal runner](/Users/ariv18uq/Desktop/toy_models_for_lop/plasticity/experiments/run_orthogonal_tasks.py); [probe utility](/Users/ariv18uq/Desktop/toy_models_for_lop/plasticity/src/probe.py); [PCA protocol design](/Users/ariv18uq/Desktop/toy_models_for_lop/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-28-pca-adversarial-design.md))

Define the primary acquisition deficit on held-out task C as

\[
D_C(\theta)=\frac1K\int_0^K\left[L_C(\theta_k)-L_C(\theta^{\mathrm{fresh}}_k)\right]dk,
\]

estimated from task-relative evaluation checkpoints over a fixed update budget K. Positive values mean worse integrated held-out learning than fresh initialization. Report the corresponding **training-loss curve**, final accuracy, steps to a task-specific fresh-derived threshold, and retention separately. Evaluate training loss at saved checkpoints, because existing online train accuracy averages predictions from changing weights. Compare both carried heads and identically initialized reset heads; give every selected C its own fresh baseline and paired minibatch order. Match task difficulty using whole fresh curves, class balance, sample counts, and target complexity. Losing beneficial transfer while remaining better than fresh is not the intended impairment claim. ([Measurement issues and proposed estimand](</Users/ariv18uq/Desktop/toy_models_for_lop/research_notes/Plasticity project publication strategy/evidence_audit.md>))

Start the causal experiment from checkpoints reached by ordinary training. Branch into the historical pool, an independent equal-sized pool from the same population, continuously refreshed samples, and duplicates of old examples. Use pool sizes **512, 2,048, and 8,192**, target angles **0°, 30°, 60°, and 90°**, and separate support shifts toward and away from dormant activation regions. Hold labels fixed in latent coordinates when transforming nuisance geometry; match fresh learning difficulty rather than assuming angle or orthogonality does so. Include stationary overtraining, repeated-A, random-B, and selected-B histories at matched update budgets. These contrasts ask whether sample reuse and support access matter beyond merely training longer or switching labels.

Use a surgical repair as an assay: for a train-silent unit, zero its readout and adjust its bias to a chosen activation quantile. Initial predictions on the historical training pool remain unchanged, while a potential learning path opens. Compare readout-zero-only, bias-only, random-unit repair, and shams matched for initial output disturbance and parameter displacement. To test mediation, block the repaired units' learning or gate access and ask whether the benefit disappears; reproduce the relevant access change independently where feasible. Compare adaptation and retention together. A repair that wins only by erasing the useful representation would weaken the claimed insight.

Freeze discovery choices before confirmation. A reasonable ample-compute starting point is **10 discovery seeds and 10 new confirmation seeds**, with separate candidate-history, predictor-fitting, and confirmation task banks. For directional searches, use at least **64 discovery and 128 untouched confirmation directions**, and report random-task distributions as well as selected extremes. Confirmation images, labels, and tasks must not select B, tune interventions, or fit the predictor. Estimate uncertainty by independent seed/history clusters, not by treating hundreds of correlated probes as independent experiments. Test held-out angles, pool sizes, and history lengths before claiming a boundary.

Use **true Shapes3D factors and CIFAR-10 tasks** as the two image families; distinguish semantic attribute changes, which can change the sampled image subset, from pure label changes. Retain width 30 as a discovery setup, then confirm at widths **120 and 512**, with a CNN and a residual network. Reserve an architecture/task family from predictor fitting. Normalized and leaky/GELU variants test the mechanism's scope; negative preactivation is not functional death for every activation. Avoid adding robot datasets unless these two families already support the claim. This buys meaningful diversity without diluting the question.

Optimization is the central alternative explanation. Sweep learning rates over **0.1×, 0.3×, 1×, 3×, and 10×** the fresh-tuned scale, include **1× and 4×** adaptation budgets, and compare equal tuning budgets for aged and fresh models. Specify optimizer-state reset versus carryover, especially for Adam/momentum; the shared runner currently rebuilds optimizers between tasks. Use function-matched parameter/input transformations when interpreting centering or scaling, and appropriate coordinate/preconditioning controls. Normalize-and-Project is essential because norm growth and effective learning-rate decline already explain apparent plasticity failures. ([NaP](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/c04d37be05ba74419d2d5705972a9d64-Paper-Conference.pdf))

Compare predictors against fresh difficulty, task similarity, gradient interference, dead fraction, norms, effective rank, residual-aligned Gram learnability, and optimization readiness. Compare interventions against full/head/partial resets, tuned L2, Shrink-and-Perturb, continual backpropagation, LayerNorm plus decay, NaP, and the spectral regularization baseline. Prevention methods must receive their own matched histories; do not insert them into an old checkpoint and call that their standard protocol. The geometry rule should improve **held-out intervention choice**, not merely prediction error: report the acquisition loss and retention cost of its chosen repair relative to the best fixed method and an oracle evaluated only after the experiment. ([Continual-backprop baseline family](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07711-7); [spectral baseline](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/prakash26b.html))

## Two weeks should determine whether this paper exists

In days 1–2, freeze a source/result manifest, reconcile stale root summaries and incompatible toy inventories, reproduce the collapse and improving-image controls, and correct probe measurement semantics. In days 3–5, run pool-refresh/support forks and test whether independently chosen image tasks show below-fresh impairment across learning rates and widths. In days 6–9, map surgical repairs, shams, and strong optimization controls; fit a simple response rule on discovery conditions. In days 10–14, lock it and test new seeds, task directions, and a distinct architecture/image family. This schedule is a recommended decision process, not a promise that the hypothesized effect exists.

**Continue only if** meaningful below-fresh deficits survive difficulty/head/learning-rate controls, trained histories produce the relevant geometry, and a prospective intervention prediction succeeds beyond the fitted toy family and improves on the nearest diagnostics. Predefine a practical effect threshold; for example, require integrated excess loss exceeding 10% of the fresh model's initial-to-final loss reduction, with paired uncertainty excluding zero. That is a project decision threshold, not a universal definition of plasticity loss. A convincing paper should contain an acquisition atlas, a causal response map, mediator controls, held-out prediction/selection, and adaptation–retention comparisons.

If the gate passes, aim for a **conditional total horizon of roughly 6–10 weeks, including the pilot**, with the remaining time spent on locked image/architecture confirmation, fair method tuning, robustness, and writing. If the geometry rule fails but selected histories reproducibly impair **new-feature generalization while preserving matched arbitrary-label fitting**, redirect once to adversarial feature-learning curricula, with independent confirmation and a representation-specific rescue. That would be a different paper, and PCA task search alone would not establish it. If neither result survives, conclude with a scoped reproducible mechanistic study/workshop paper. **Stop the major-paper route** if the effect requires tiny fixed-pool networks, disappears under optimization controls, or merely removes positive transfer. More datasets cannot repair that failure.

The prospective paper title is **“Task Geometry Predicts When Plasticity Repairs Work.”** A conditional abstract, to use only after validation, is: “We study why the same plasticity intervention produces different outcomes across future tasks. Controlled checkpoint experiments separate finite-sample silence, future activation access, and task-aligned learning requirements. We identify an intervention-response boundary and test its predictions on unseen task families and architectures. Matched counterfactual repairs isolate the proposed mechanism, while fresh-model and optimization controls separate impaired acquisition from task difficulty and effective learning-rate changes. The resulting rule selects interventions while accounting for retention.” These sentences describe the intended evidential package; **none is a claim that those results have already been obtained**.

## Conclusion

The strongest use of this project is to turn precise toy access geometry into a testable decision about future learning. Its negative controls are an advantage because they force the claim to explain preserved plasticity as well as failure. I would choose ICML, NeurIPS, or ICLR only after this empirical decision; the contribution must earn the venue through a causal, prospective result, rather than through the breadth of the experimental platform.
