# Literature and publication positioning for the plasticity project

## 1. What is already established, and which work most threatens novelty?

### Takeaway

The closest work is **Lyle et al., _Disentangling the Causes of Plasticity Loss in Neural Networks_**, first posted February 2024; the PMLR record labels its proceedings publication 2025, whereas the paper itself says CoLLAs 2024. It already discusses task-switch common gradient pushes, preactivation drift, target offsets, normalization, leaky ReLU, and the dependence of unit revival on a changed input distribution. A generic story that norm growth, dead units, or rank loss causes LoP would substantially overlap a mature literature. The strongest new direction is a quantitative, experimentally established boundary on when those mechanisms occur and when specific interventions reverse their effects.

### Cited Findings

- **Dohare, Hernandez-Garcia, Lan, Rahman, Mahmood, Sutton, _Loss of plasticity in deep continual learning_, Nature 632 (21 August 2024).** Demonstrates long-stream supervised and RL degradation; analyzes weight magnitude, dormant units and representation rank; introduces continual backpropagation and compares L2 and Shrink-and-Perturb. This establishes both phenomenon and baseline family. Full article opened. — [Nature article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07711-7)
- **Clare Lyle, Zeyu Zheng, Khimya Khetarpal, Hado van Hasselt, Razvan Pascanu, James Martens, Will Dabney, _Disentangling the Causes of Plasticity Loss in Neural Networks_.** PMLR 274:750–783 (2025), proceedings of the third Conference on Lifelong Learning Agents; first arXiv submission February 2024. The full text separates independent mechanisms, studies regression-target offsets and abrupt label changes, and notes that dead units remain frozen unless their input distribution moves suitably. It describes common negative preactivation pushes after task switches, including norm-reducing feature updates; leaky ReLU weakens rather than abolishes analogous eNTK pathology. Layer normalization plus decay is its broad empirical remedy. Full HTML opened, especially Sections 3 and appendix classification dynamics. — [Full paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.18762); [proceedings metadata](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v274/lyle25a.html)
- **Lyle et al., _Understanding Plasticity in Neural Networks_, ICML 2023.** Links plasticity to loss-landscape curvature and shows saturation is unnecessary. Proceedings page and abstract opened; detailed claims should use the paper PDF before quotation. — [PMLR 202](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/lyle23b.html)
- **Lyle et al., _Normalization and effective learning rates in reinforcement learning_, NeurIPS 2024.** Shows normalization can revive dormant ReLUs, but parameter-norm growth can reduce effective learning rate. Proposes Normalize-and-Project. A normalization-versus-norm explanation or revival observation alone is therefore insufficient. Full proceedings PDF opened. — [NeurIPS paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/c04d37be05ba74419d2d5705972a9d64-Paper-Conference.pdf)
- **Lee et al., _PLASTIC: Improving Input and Label Plasticity for Sample Efficient Reinforcement Learning_, NeurIPS 2023.** Already separates input adaptation from evolving input–output relations and tests both using controlled CIFAR-10 experiments, followed by RL evaluation. Simply distinguishing input drift from output drift is not new. Full proceedings PDF opened. — [NeurIPS record](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2023/hash/c464fc4516aca4e68f2a14e67c6f0402-Abstract-Conference.html)
- **Prakash, He, Guo, Tiwari, Serapio, Tao, Greenwald, Konidaris, _Spectral Collapse Drives Loss of Plasticity in Deep Continual Learning_, ICML 2026, PMLR 306.** Goes beyond rank correlations: Section 4 explicitly decomposes new-task residuals into Gram eigendirections and derives a finite-budget fast-subspace condition; relates the loss-weighted Gram to GGN curvature; evaluates feature effective-rank plus L2 regularization. Thus residual-aligned NTK learnability and finite training budgets are already analyzed. Full 48-page PDF opened; Sections 4–5 inspected. — [Proceedings](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/prakash26b.html); [full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.22335)
- **Joudaki et al., _Barriers for Learning in an Evolving World: Mathematical Understanding of Loss of Plasticity_, ICLR 2026.** Defines invariant LoP manifolds; characterizes frozen and cloned-unit traps; connects rank compression/neural collapse to their emergence. Section 5 identifies the precise architectural/data conditions for manifold stability as an open issue. Full February 2026 revision opened; ICLR status cross-checked against the authors' research page. — [Full paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.00304v2); [author publication listing](https://pr-mlr-shield-prod.apple.com/updates/apple-at-iclr-2026)
- **Shin and Karniadakis, _Trainability of ReLU networks and Data-dependent Initialization_, arXiv:1907.09696 (2019; v2 March 2020).** Defines death on a bounded input domain, proves shallow dead units cannot revive under gradient training, and distinguishes tentative from permanent death in deeper networks. Sections 2–3 and Lemma 1 inspected in the full PDF. The repository's sample-silent versus support-inactive distinction is useful operational care, but is not alone a new foundational discovery. — [Full PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.09696)
- **Wang, Srinivasa, Chen, Liu, Payani, Zhang, _Predicting Plasticity in Deep Continual Learning: A Theoretical Perspective_, arXiv:2605.09044 (9 May 2026).** Constructs label-dependent failures of representation/eNTK rank; defines future k-step optimization gain; proposes optimization readiness from gradient strength and reliability; tests checkpoint ranking on slowly changing regression and permuted MNIST. This directly overlaps both proxy falsification and a generic new trainability diagnostic. Full HTML opened, including theory and experimental definitions. No accepted venue verified. — [Full paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09044v1)
- **Rosseau, Müller, Nowé, _Preserving Plasticity in Continual Learning via Dynamical Isometry_, arXiv:2606.09762 (8 June 2026).** Promotes layer Jacobian isometry, introduces AdamO, and analyzes regularizer-mediated revival of dead ReLUs. Distinguishes task-agnostic isotropy from task-specific specialization. Full HTML opened; no accepted venue independently verified. — [Full paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.09762v1)
- **Kastner, De La Vega, Farahmand, _SingularClip: Preventing Spectral Collapse to Maintain Plasticity in Continual and Reinforcement Learning_, arXiv:2608.18319 (18 August 2026).** Studies increasing singular-value anisotropy and periodic singular-value clipping. Full HTML opened. No accepted venue verified. This is a current comparator for any norm/rank control method. — [Full paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18319v1)
- **_What Can Grokking Teach Us About Learning Under Nonstationarity?_, arXiv:2507.20057 (26 July 2025).** Connects primacy bias with delayed feature learning and proposes effective-learning-rate rewarming, evaluated in grokking, image warm-starting and RL. Uses activation-pattern and feature-covariance changes to measure representation adaptation. Full HTML opened; accepted venue not verified. — [Full paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.20057v1)

### Inferences

- **Highest overlap risk:** the existing shared-push narrative is closest to _Disentangling_, not merely the broad Nature paper. For a new diagnostic paper, Wang et al. is the most immediate collision; for a toy invariant-trap paper, Joudaki et al. is the immediate collision; for a kernel-alignment paper, Prakash et al. is the immediate collision.
- Do not pitch “centering fixes LoP,” “rank is insufficient,” “input and output shifts differ,” “dead neurons can recover after input shift,” or “feature learning and memorization differ” as the main novelty. Each requires a stronger, new result beyond the cited precursors.
- Do not characterize the Nature paper's random-component conclusion as an established impossibility theorem. Its scope is empirical; later normalization, projection and spectral approaches provide additional plasticity mechanisms.

### Gaps

- This is a focused novelty search as of **28 October 2026**, not an exhaustive systematic review. I found no directly matching quantitative support-geometry intervention-reversal law, but this is not proof that none exists.
- Metadata conflict for _Disentangling_: cite arXiv 2024 / CoLLAs 2024 where using paper chronology, and explain PMLR's 2025 publication date rather than silently assigning one date.
- Two additional near-neighbors surfaced but were not examined in full: _The Dual Nature of Plasticity Loss in Deep Continual Learning: Dissection and Mitigation_ (NeurIPS 2025; training failure versus test degradation, neural collapse/FTLE), and _Activation Function Design Sustains Plasticity in Continual Learning_ (authors' code labels ICLR 2026). Do not make detailed claims about them without reading their full papers. — [NeurIPS record](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/6489f2c6ac6420124fcef2a489615a97-Abstract-Conference.html); [authors' repository](https://github.com/lute47lillo/activations_plasticity)

## 2. Which contribution best uses this repository's comparative advantage?

### Takeaway

**Rank 1: an experimental law governing entry into, and recovery from, plasticity failure as a function of task-support geometry.** The key result must be a causal, quantitative regime boundary that predicts intervention reversals on unseen conditions. The support-extremum formula is standard geometry; the scientific contribution would be showing when history actually drives a learner across that boundary and how the boundary predicts future learning beyond existing explanations.

### Cited Findings

- The repository explicitly computes the whole-support preactivation maximum `w·mu + ||A^T w|| + b`, distinguishes sample silence from support-certified inactivity, and treats tiny vectors separately from directional rotation. This provides unusually precise interventions compared with only logging aggregate dormant-unit counts. — [local geometry implementation](/Users/ariv18uq/Desktop/toy_models_for_lop/plasticity/src/unit_geometry.py:1)
- Controlled orthogonal task runners and geometry tasks already exist, together with fresh-model probe and empirical NTK utilities. These are natural tools for checkpoint branching and matched counterfactual experiments. Their existence is not evidence that a paper-level result has already been established. — [orthogonal runner](/Users/ariv18uq/Desktop/toy_models_for_lop/plasticity/experiments/run_orthogonal_tasks.py); [task construction](/Users/ariv18uq/Desktop/toy_models_for_lop/plasticity/tasks/orthogonal_halfspaces.py); [probe utility](/Users/ariv18uq/Desktop/toy_models_for_lop/plasticity/src/probe.py); [NTK utility](/Users/ariv18uq/Desktop/toy_models_for_lop/plasticity/src/ntk.py)
- The PCA-adversarial design already specifies identical checkpoint branching, fresh-task difficulty controls, paired head initialization, validation-only selection and separate confirmation probes. That design supports more reliable falsification than selecting a harmful task and reporting its own training failure. — [local design](/Users/ariv18uq/Desktop/toy_models_for_lop/docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-28-pca-adversarial-design.md)

### Inferences

**Preferred headline hypothesis:** task history need not remove generic capacity; it can concentrate trainable directions so that the *next task's support* determines whether capacity is accessible. Whether this becomes interesting depends on a demonstrated regime boundary, not just on an illustration of a dead ReLU.

The decisive experiment is a checkpoint fork with two independently manipulated axes: future input support geometry and required future decision rule. Balance fresh-model difficulty and preserve sample size, label balance, training budget, target complexity and prior-task fit. Across suitable branches, also match or control parameter norms, dormant fraction and representation/eNTK spectra. Then test whether the **same intervention changes sign across supports**: for example, a norm/centering/revival intervention helps one family but impairs another, while a support-based prediction made before training gets the direction and approximate magnitude right. The exact intervention must follow pilot evidence; do not assume centering reversal exists.

An adequate explanation would need all of the following:

1. A boundary expressed through support margins, probability of activating previously silent units, or available task-aligned directions. Use the analytic ellipsoid only where the assumptions hold; a finite image sample cannot certify population support.
2. **Induction**, showing ordinary learning trajectories cross the boundary under controlled histories, rather than manually placing neurons in a trap.
3. **Rescue**, showing a targeted support change or parameter intervention crosses it back and improves matched fresh-relative adaptation. Measure both train loss and held-out generalization.
4. **Specificity**, separating support exclusion from feature norm collapse, negative bias, optimizer-state effects and simple insufficient learning rate.
5. **Prediction**, fitting any empirical mapping on one group and testing it on unseen angles, shifts, widths, training lengths, architectures, and at least one real factor family.
6. **Comparison**, showing additional explanatory/predictive value beyond task-aligned Gram residual mass, NaP/effective learning rate and optimization readiness. These are substantive nearest baselines, not just L2 and raw rank.

**Rank 2: adversarial curricula that impair future *new-feature* learning despite retained memorization ability.** This is potentially more distinctive if the geometric law fails. The main result would be a reproducible generalization–memorization dissociation: selected histories make a network fall below fresh initialization on held-out semantic factors, while retaining matched arbitrary-label fitting ability; a representation-targeted rescue reverses that gap without a generic capacity increase. Select histories/probes on training and validation, then confirm on unseen semantic factors, image sets and architectures. A PCA-search tool alone, or loss of positive transfer while remaining better than fresh, does not establish this claim. Compare to effective-learning-rate rewarming and DASH, which already address feature learning and warm-start generalization.

**Rank 3: intervention rankings depend on the task family, and standard LoP benchmarks therefore mislead method selection.** A benchmark paper needs a strong scientific conclusion such as consistent ranking reversals between randomized-label tasks, pixel permutations, and new semantic factors after fresh-difficulty matching. Release task generators, checkpoint probes and diagnostic protocol as supporting artifacts. A collection of Shapes3D/PCA/robot streams without such a conclusion is unlikely to be sufficient on its own. This is weaker as the default angle because multiple prior works already show mechanism- and benchmark-dependent remedies.

### Gaps

- I did not independently audit all saved results; numerical findings belong to the separate empirical audit. No claim here treats existing plans or code as completed scientific evidence.
- A one-layer bounded-support death result does not automatically transfer to deeper networks: upstream representations can move. Biased, momentum, weight decay and adaptive-optimizer cases also need explicit scope.
- No guarantee of an intervention reversal or useful cross-domain law exists yet. It is a proposed discriminating test, not a discovery.

## 3. What evidence would justify pursuing or abandoning the paper angle?

### Takeaway

Treat novelty as a go/no-go decision around a small set of discriminating results. More datasets and more seeds can establish reliability, but cannot turn an already established mechanism into a new contribution.

### Cited Findings

- Prior empirical counterexamples already challenge norm, rank, dormancy and update-norm explanations individually; a rank-decrease/accuracy-increase plot is therefore a useful sanity check, not a standalone paper claim. Full text contains a dedicated counterexamples section. — [Lewandowski et al., _Directions of Curvature as an Explanation for Loss of Plasticity_](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.00246v3)
- The strongest spectral competitor already formulates successful finite-budget training through new-task residual projection, while the strongest prediction competitor tests future optimization gains directly. Consequently, an additional correlation must demonstrate a causal or generalization advantage over those baselines. — [Prakash et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.22335); [Wang et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.09044v1)
- A recent ICLR 2026 paper studies abundant replay, rank-based resets and averaging for image and language tasks; it reinforces that practical continual-learning comparison can separate plasticity from retention rather than conflating them. Only its accepted proceedings abstract was opened here. — [Cho et al., _Forget Forgetting_](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/17fc58b1ba92abbb72c5f457e140d851-Abstract-Conference.html)

### Inferences

- **Pursue the geometry paper** if there is a substantial fresh-relative deficit, a clean manipulated regime boundary, at least one prospective rescue/selection prediction that existing metrics miss, and replication across unseen support/task conditions plus a meaningful image setting. A sign reversal is especially persuasive but not logically mandatory if another sharp causal prediction generalizes.
- **Kill or redirect it** if the result disappears after matching fresh-task difficulty or effective learning rate, if only hand-engineered traps work, if the claimed law merely restates the support function or NTK residual formula, or if prediction fails outside the fitted toy family. In that case, present the work as a careful mechanistic study or redirect to adversarial feature-learning curricula; do not compensate by adding unrelated datasets.
- **Pursue the feature-learning angle** only if memorization remains demonstrably available while fresh-relative new-feature generalization fails, and controlled rescue identifies why. **Kill it** if the discrepancy is explained by head initialization, unequal task difficulty, learning budgets, leakage, or a failure shared by fresh networks.
- **Pursue a benchmark/method-selection angle** only if controlled task-family variation reveals stable, practically consequential ranking reversals and a rule for selecting methods. **Kill it** if it merely reproduces that L2/normalization/resets work differently across existing benchmarks.
- For ICML/NeurIPS/ICLR positioning, the credible package is one strong claim plus a causal experimental chain, architecture scope, fair strong baselines and public reproducibility. Venue choice cannot substitute for the missing result, and acceptance cannot be predicted from repository breadth.

### Gaps

- No venue deadlines, acceptance probabilities or future review outcomes were assumed.
- An exhaustive late-2026 literature update remains necessary before writing a novelty claim or submitting; this scoped review already found several 2026 papers that invalidate plausible older pitches.
